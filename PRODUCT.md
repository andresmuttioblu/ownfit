# Ownfit — product context

## Register
product

## Platform
web (mobile-first: used on a phone while getting dressed; works on desktop for catalog review)

## What it is
An open-source, self-hosted wardrobe app. Film your closet once, get a clean, tagged catalog, then tap what you're doing today and get outfits from clothes you actually own. Also: a fitting room ("should I buy this?"), and later a buy list, a sell-or-donate list and travel packing.

## Who uses it
One person per deployment, on their own Vercel + Neon. Any wardrobe: menswear, womenswear or mixed; no gender switch. Opened on a phone in the morning, often in a hurry, sometimes in low light.

## Purpose
Make getting dressed quick and confident with what you already own. Pull, not push: the app never nags.

## Personality
Calm and quiet. A tidy closet: lots of air, the clothes are the color, the app stays out of the way. Plain, kind words.

## Anti-references
- Shopping apps: sale badges, carousels, "add to cart" energy.
- Generic SaaS: grey dashboards, stat cards, admin feel.
- Luxury cliché: black and gold, hairline serifs, "premium" posing.
- Cute or childish: pastel mascots, rounded-everything, emoji-heavy UI.

## Design principles
1. The garments are the color. The interface is black, grey and white; selection is shown by inverting, not by a brand color.
2. Truthful images. Real photos of real garments on white; never decorate or alter them.
3. One-hand, one-thumb. Primary actions sit low and are big enough to hit half-awake.
4. Every state says what to do next: loading, empty, missing pieces, errors.
5. Familiar over clever. Standard controls, consistent everywhere.
6. Uncluttered. One big choice per row, no captions or boxes that don't earn their place; icons, illustrations and motion where they help. Empty states are a little fun.

## Accessibility
WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1 body text), visible focus, 44px touch targets on the phone, respects reduced motion and the phone's light/dark setting.
